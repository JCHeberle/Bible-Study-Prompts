## User Manual and Framework Guide

Welcome to the apostolic, thought-for-thought Bible study framework. This repository contains a rigorous, 12,000+ word system prompt designed to force Large Language Models (LLMs) like Grok, Gemini, Claude, and ChatGPT to analyze biblical texts strictly through the grammatical-historical lens of the 30-95 AD Apostolic era.

But why is such a massive, restrictive prompt necessary? Why can't we simply ask an AI to "interpret this bible verse based on the 1st century"? This guide explains the core philosophy behind the prompt, why certain words and concepts are excluded, and how to get the most out of your AI-assisted study.

### The Gravity of Default Training Weights

Large Language Models are prediction engines trained on vast oceans of human text. When it comes to biblical interpretation, the overwhelming majority of English texts in an AI's training data—commentaries, sermons, systematic theologies, and academic articles from the last 500+ years—teach post-Enlightenment, exchange-based, or clinical theology.

The AI's neural pathways are fundamentally hardwired with what we call the **"Majority Report."** This includes:

* **Commercial/Transactional Logic:** Treating salvation as a literal financial exchange (e.g., "paying off a debt to a distant God").  
* **Post-95 AD Frameworks:** Using 16th-century Reformation concepts or 11th-century Anselmian Satisfaction theories as the default lens for reading a 1st-century text.  
* **Clinical/Analytical Language:** Describing spiritual realities using modern biological, clinical, or systematic terms rather than the organic, relational vocabulary used by the Apostles.

Because this post-95 AD view is statistically dominant, it exerts a massive gravitational pull. The AI will default to it every single time unless explicitly and forcefully restrained.

### The Danger: The "Illusion of Compliance"

One of the greatest challenges in using AI for biblical exegesis is how easily a human user's phrasing can trigger this majority report. If a user asks a question using modern theological shorthand, the AI's default weights will instantly flood the response.

Worse still is the **Illusion of Compliance**. If you give an AI a simple rule to "sound like the 1st century," it will easily adopt the *vocabulary* of the Apostolic era (using words like "redemption," "propitiation," or "flesh"). However, beneath that approved vocabulary, it will silently smuggle in its default modern logic. It will use 1st-century words to describe a modern commercial exchange. The output will look correct, but the underlying spiritual mechanics will be entirely anachronistic.

### The Core Purpose: Restricting AI Probabilities

At its most fundamental level, an LLM generates text based on mathematical probabilities—connecting words and concepts that appear most frequently together in its training data.

**The core purpose of this framework is to artificially restrict that probability space.** By utilizing this prompt, we isolate the AI's predictive capabilities exclusively to the Biblical texts themselves. We build a walled garden that forces the AI to connect concepts using *only* the grammatical realities of Koine Greek, Biblical Hebrew, and Aramaic.

Inside this framework, the AI is explicitly forbidden from drawing mathematical or semantic connections using any theology, creeds, or systematic consensus developed after 95 AD. The text interprets the text, limiting the interpretation entirely to the Apostolic lens and ensuring that the scriptural reality remains untouched by centuries of later human philosophy.

### The Semantic Firewall

To stop an LLM from acting like a 21st-century systematic theologian, the rules cannot just be suggestions; they must be absolute constraints. Think of these exclusions not as censorship, but as guardrails on a steep mountain highway. Here is how this firewall protects your study:

1. **The Principle of Unaltered Consistency:** Our founding presupposition is that God's Word does not contradict itself. Therefore, we do not need later theological "shortcuts" to make verses fit together. This framework forces the AI to do the hard work of harmonizing the text as it was originally written, ensuring that every analysis is an inevitable consequence of the text itself, rather than a forced doctrinal opinion.  
2. **Counteracting AI "Drift":** Our framework acts as a deliberate counter-measure to AI drift, forcing the AI off the "broad path" of internet consensus and back onto the "narrow way" of the apostolic text.  
3. **Avoiding Historical Shortcuts:** Throughout history, well-meaning scholars have invented complex vocabulary to explain away apparent tensions in Scripture. While popular, these terms often subtly redefine the original Greek and Hebrew texts. By omitting these modern terms, we prevent the AI from importing centuries of external baggage into your study session.

## The Execution: Operational Modes & Staging

Once the Agent is built, the chat window itself must remain completely sterile. The framework operates under three distinct modes. You must trigger the mode you want to use.

### 1\. Dialogue / Chat Mode (Default)

If no specific bracketed command is given, the AI operates in default Dialogue Mode. There are no mechanical staging or rigid headers here. The AI will speak clearly, elegantly, and warmly while strictly applying the 30–95 AD lens to answer your questions or discuss concepts.

### 2\. Lexical / Usage Study Mode

* **Trigger:** `[ACTIVATE: Lexical Mode]`  
* **Purpose:** For deep-dive analysis on a single Greek or Hebrew word/phrase across a specific corpus. It scopes occurrences, runs a mini linguistic audit (mismatches/compressed force), and provides a synthesis of the word's living range and force.

### 3\. Strict Mode (The 3-Stage Exegesis Protocol)

* **Trigger:** `[ACTIVATE: Strict Mode]`  
* **Purpose:** This is the core engine for full passage exegesis. It strictly prevents the AI from rushing to theological conclusions by forcing a mechanical, gated process.

**CRITICAL RULE FOR STRICT MODE:** To prevent "Context Bloat" and attention mechanism drift, **each group in Stage 2 and Stage 3 must be executed in a brand-new, isolated chat window.**

* **Stage 1: Structural Outline**  
    
  * **Input:** The NA28 Greek (or Hebrew for OT) text for the entire passage.  
  * **Action:** The AI produces a broad, non-narrative structural skeleton noting major discourse movements without running exposition.  
  * **Halt:** The AI stops and waits for approval.


* **Stage 2: Linguistic Audit**  
    
  * **Preparation:** Divide the passage into small groups (usually 3 verses, adjusting as needed for the final group).  
  * **Input (New Chat):** Provide *only* the specific verses for the current group in both NA28 Greek and NASB/ESV English.  
  * **Action:** The AI generates Part A (Translation Mismatches) and Part B (Compressed/Flattened Meaning) data.  
  * **Halt & Reset:** Once the AI finishes the group, **start a new chat**. In the new chat, issue the approval command for the previous group and provide the text for the next group. Repeat until all groups are audited.


* **Stage 3: Progressive Exposition (The Puzzle Pieces)**  
    
  * **Preparation:** You will now build the final exposition piece by piece.  
  * **Input (New Chat):** For each group, provide the Stage 1 Structural Outline, the NA28 & NASB/ESV text for the current group, and the approved Stage 2 data for that exact group.  
  * **Action:** The AI will generate a seamless, formatted exposition draft (a "puzzle piece") strictly based on the audited data.  
  * **Halt & Reset:** Save the generated piece. Start a new chat for the next group.

**Final Synthesis:** Once all Stage 3 puzzle pieces are generated, bring the entire compiled draft into a final Gemini Canvas session (or iteratively with Grok). With the Framework operating in the background, you will collaboratively polish the document to ensure the narrative seams are invisible and the teaching voice remains firmly anchored in the first century.

## Navigating Nuances and Edge Cases

As you use this prompt, especially in small groups or Discord study sessions, you might encounter a few edge cases:

* **"This feels restrictive\!"** It is completely normal to feel a bit of friction when you first use this framework. However, these exclusions are purely defensive tools. They are not designed to ban ideas, but to protect the 1st-century lens. Think of it like a water filter: it doesn't restrict your water; it simply removes the sediment so you can drink it pure.  
* **"Does this framework argue for a specific denomination?"** No. This framework never argues for or against any specific modern theological view. It simply enforces the axiom that the text must agree with itself. Users who prefer to view Scripture through the lens of modern denominational frameworks will naturally self-select out of using this prompt.  
* **"Will this work on my preferred AI model?"** Yes. The framework relies on foundational prompting logic and manual context management rather than model-specific code. However, models with advanced reasoning capabilities and large context windows (like Gemini Pro or Claude) are highly recommended, as they are much better at adhering to the strict operational halts required in the 3-Stage Exegesis Protocol.

## Best Practices for Usage

* **Share Freely:** You can confidently share this prompt in forums, study groups, or with students. Because the framework is strictly governed by Scripture interpreting Scripture, it provides a highly rigorous, objective baseline for group discussion.  
* **Reviewing Outputs:** When reviewing the AI's answers, always check them against this core manual. Ask yourself: Did the AI successfully logically "AND" the passages together, or did it try to sneak in a modern theological shortcut?
