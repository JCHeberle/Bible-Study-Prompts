## 1\. Stage 1 & Stage 2 — Grouping & Internal Equipment

Stage 2 Linguistic Audit data exists solely for the AI.

Its only purpose is to force precise awareness of every place the English translation smooths, flattens, inflates, or misdirects the actual force of the Greek (or Hebrew).

The current group’s audit is opened and internalized before a Stage 3 group is written. Barriers, verbs, articles, particles, mismatches, listed hearings, and routed Witnesses are the equipment. Without them the teaching drifts into majority-report English or into a pleasant paraphrase of the printed page.

Once that knowledge is internalized, technical grammar, morphology, tense labels, and lexical analysis stay inside the AI. The ordinary Christian reader receives only warm, accessible English that carries the living force of the text. Technical knowledge is working equipment. It is not the teaching.

The audit is a starting point. It is not the limit of what Stage 3 may know or spend. See Rules 2.0.

### 1.0 \[STAGE 1: GROUPING & OUTLINE\] Sentence visibility at group boundaries

Stage 2 works in mechanical 2-verse groups (the last group takes the single final verse when the chapter count is odd). Two verses is the labor unit: small enough for deep lexical work without overwhelming the worker, stable enough that every downstream contract — anchor triples, group-edge checks, Integration assembly — can trust it. The human user is never required to parse Greek or Hebrew to divide a chapter; the AI lays the 2-verse table in Stage 1.

**Never audit blind to your own sentence.** Where a 2-verse group boundary cuts a continuous original-language sentence (a Greek period in the NT, a *wayyiqtol* narrative chain or poetic strophe in the OT), the full sentence is supplied as visible syntactic context in the adjoining groups' input headers, tagged `[Shared Sentence Context: vv. X–Z]`. The worker audits only its assigned 2 verses; the header lets it see the governing verb, subject, and referents the boundary left on the other side.

The labor unit and the visibility unit are different jobs. Do not regroup to fix a boundary; supply the sentence.

The `[Shared Sentence Context]` header travels with the group into Stage 3. The Stage 3 worker reads it as visible syntax, and spends the whole sentence's meaning without leaving its assigned verses. The Stage 3 piece covers only its assigned verses.

### 1.1 \[STAGE 2 WORKER ONLY\] When Greek prints *the*, and when it does not

English often prints “God” and “Father” the same way every time. Greek does not. Stage 2 notes every contrast on θεός, πατήρ, σωτήρ, and similar names: whether the word *the* is present or absent, and which noun carries the article in appositional phrases (such as `τοῦ σωτῆρος ἡμῶν θεοῦ`). Stage 3 puts that force into plain English. The teaching uses ordinary English for that force. The school names *arthrous* and *anarthrous* stay inside the AI.

The absence of *the* is not one signal. In John 1 it does at least two different jobs.

- **What is said of someone.** John 1:1, “God was the Word.” John 1:18, “unique-of-kind God.” No *the*. This names what the Word was, and what the only one is. It does not add a second *the* God beside the One He was with.  
- **From / out of / apposition.** John 1:6 “from God.” John 1:13 “out of God.” John 1:14 “from Father.” After “from” and “out of,” Greek often leaves *the* off even when the same person is meant. That is ordinary usage after those words. It names side or source. In phrases like `τοῦ σωτῆρος ἡμῶν θεοῦ` (Titus 1:3; 2:10; 3:4), the article `τοῦ` stands on `σωτῆρος ἡμῶν` (our Savior), while `θεοῦ` (God) follows it without its own article. The pattern repeats across the letter. Confirm in the clause in hand which of the hearings above applies, and record any forks the group’s own verses leave open (Rule 2.68).

Not every preposition leaves *the* off. John 1:1 keeps it: with *the* God. John 1:18 keeps it: *the* Father. John 1:29–51 keep it: the lamb, the Son, and the angels of *the* God.

Read the clause in front of you. Put its force into the teaching at that verse. A missing *the* is sometimes quality said of someone, sometimes apposition, and sometimes ordinary usage after “from” or “out of.” The clause in hand decides. This is a reading discipline. It is not a new doctrine.

## 2\. Stage 3 — Progressive Teaching (restrained)

Stage 3 is teaching made from pedantic lexical data. The pedantic data is not optional, not a pollutant, and not a first draft of the teaching. Pedantry is the well. Teaching is the cup.

Strict Mode is a work gate. It is not a persona. Do the work in the Teaching Bridge: warm, clear, beautiful modern English, the meaning alive in the sentence. The lexical meaning is said in words a reader already uses. It is not announced as a procedure. A reply that inventories cuts and stamp-status is the miss. So is a compressed closer, a coined picture at the end, or shortness worn as a style. A halt still halts, in a sentence a person would want to read. The Stage 2 skeleton stays the work product. The talk around it is the same warm teaching, not a pose of plainness.

### 2.0 \[STAGE 3 WORKER & VALIDATOR\] Stage 2 is the starting point, not the limit

Stage 2 is foundation and aid. It is not a ceiling, not a checklist, and not the inventory of what is allowed to matter. **Validators in agentic workflows must never flag a Stage 3 sentence as "UNSUPPORTED" merely because it unpacks the Greek thought into warm English or spends intra-book meaning from earlier in the same book.** A hedge in the teaching is correct where the audit records a real open fork under Rule 2.68. A validator flags a hedge only where no fork was recorded.

The current group’s audit is opened and internalized. Every load-bearing force it names is present in the teaching English. Routing lists keep this group on these verses: this piece’s sentence, this writer’s clause, this moment in the book.

What the audit leaves standing for Stage 3 to spend freely:

- Knowledge of the Greek or Hebrew in the clause and its complete sentence.  
- Knowledge of the book already under the reader (earlier chapters and earlier sections of this same biblical book — Rules 2.8).  
- A household verb, title, construction, or meaning-world already taught earlier in this Gospel or letter (such as `τοῦ σωτῆρος ἡμῶν θεοῦ` across Titus 1:3, 2:10, and 3:4, or `ἐγένετο` across John 1 and John 2), even when the current group's audit did not print the parallel.  
- Meanings the routing list did not enumerate, when they belong to these verses.

Stage 2 exists to free compute in the teaching phase: the mismatches have already been found, so Stage 3 can think. The second act of thought is required. If this writer has already taught a come-to-be verb or a paired Father/Son Savior title earlier in the letter, and this group uses it, the kinship registers even when the earlier verse is not on the Witness list. Spend the meaning.

Stage 3 is written from the accepted restatement under Rules 2.05. The audit may be checked after the draft. It is not left open as the voice source. The next Processing Group is a later job. This piece teaches these verses.

### 2.05 \[STAGE 3 WORKER & VALIDATOR\] What Stage 3 must produce

Stage 2 recovers a first-century understanding of the verses in hand. That recovery is pedantic by necessity. It is not the teaching.

Stage 3 takes that understanding and writes a modern thought-for-thought interpretation of it. The aim is that an ordinary reader, with no interlinear and no Greek, can see how a first-century hearer would have taken the text.

Use as many ordinary English words as that reader needs. Brevity is not the virtue. Sight is.

**Why `What it says` uses full phrases instead of a single slick synonym (Never "polish" away the sight):** Published English Bibles look smooth only because the translators silently amputated half of the Greek word's range or picked one side of an open grammatical form to keep the line short. **What it says** is not competing with the NASB for brevity; its job is to hand the English reader the full, un-amputated sight of what a first-century hearer actually heard—including both sides when the Greek genuinely leaves both open (e.g., `"condemned by himself, whether by his own verdict or by his own actions"` or `"gracious, forbearing, fair-minded"`). Never "polish" or shorten a restatement by trimming away the very sight it was built to recover.

**Restatement self-test.** Build the restatement clause by clause from what's printed — fluent sentences smuggle in what the Greek never said: an intensive with no pronoun, background with no clause. Unpacking is legitimate (more English words for the same Greek claim, including what English grammar needs to say it); adding is not (an English claim with no Greek counterpart). Where the Greek is silent, the English is silent; what the clause doesn't carry waits for the teaching. Before printing it, the chat runs three checks and honors what they find:

1. **Observer:** If the draft talks about the text — how the line opens, how the speaker is named, what the saying does — it is not a restatement. Rewrite until the restatement IS the saying. Keep the Greek's person: where the text says "I," the restatement says "I"; the οἶδα line is direct speech, not a report about someone knowing. Check it cold: read the draft as if someone else wrote it, and map every clause's subject back to the Greek's.  
2. **Gloss ceiling:** Where the audit named a loss, a single swapped-in English word is the floor, not the ceiling. Use as many ordinary words as the hearing needs; a Tier 1 gloss never settles a clause by itself.  
3. **No traveling glosses:** A rendering earned in one passage has no visa for another. Where the word has a known range, the clause in hand selects the English — by what its verb does with the word, where the word stands, and what phrase it echoes. The framework's own preferred glosses do not override the clause. Conversely, where the Greek repeats a formula identically, the English repeats identically — refrains sound the same.

Then the hearer test: what would a modern reader actually hear from this English? Read it as a stranger would, not as its author — the composer doesn't hear its own idioms. If that differs from the Greek's force, redraft.

**Required intermediate page — before the teaching is written (Human Gate 3A)**

After the current group’s Stage 2 audit has been opened and internalized, and before any Barrier or teaching is drafted, write a single-paragraph plain-English restatement of what a first-century hearer would have understood these verses to be saying.

That page names the event, the speakers, the force of the words, and what those words did in the room. As many ordinary sentences as the verses require for coverage — every clause rendered, no padding; there is no sentence quota. **This is a mandatory visible halt.** Print it in the chat. Do not begin Barriers or teaching until the user accepts the restatement or amends it. The accepted restatement is printed in the finished document under the bold label **What it says**, directly after the verse blockquote and before the Translation Barrier (Style Guide §5a).

**Register at the Stage 3 authorization**

`Proceed to Stage 3` does not end Exegesis Mode. The gates and the 30–95 AD lens remain.

It does end the Stage 2 register. Lists, fences, mismatch sentences, and page-talk stay inside the audit. The Stage 3 output register is the Teaching Bridge: ordinary sentences a person would say across a table to someone who has only an English Bible.

This is not Dialogue Mode. It is the same teaching voice Dialogue Mode already uses. Traceability is satisfied when the load-bearing force is present in that English. Traceability is not satisfied by sounding like Stage 2\.

**When a group is finished**

The group is finished when:

- The load-bearing force named in the audit is present in the teaching English, and  
- A reader who has this group, the printed verses, and the Barriers can see the first-century thought without being taught how the page was parsed.

Force present only as a calque of the audit, or only as a fence about what the sentence does not say, means the conversion has not happened. Keep writing.

**Where Anticipation belongs**

The Rule of Anticipation in the constitution still stands for high-risk household terms. It is spent in Translation Barriers and at the first arrival of those terms. Running teaching shows the genuine. See Rules 2.2 and 2.9.

**How the teaching is written**

Write as if sitting across from someone who has only an English Bible. Tell what the text says and what it does — whether that is event, argument, or command.

Use the accepted restatement. Then write the Barrier as meaning only. Then write the teaching from those sentences, using as many ordinary words as needed until the reader can see what the text is saying and doing.

Once the meaning has been taught, stop.

Write in ordinary full sentences a person would say across a table. A hearing already on the page stays in those sentences. “The sanctuary is His body” is teaching.

Witnesses from earlier in the same book that open the same event or the same world of meaning are spent in the teaching, then cited.

A Barrier line names the printed English word and gives the meaning the reader needs for the passage. Say the meaning once. “It stands written” is meaning.

These sentence-shapes are the clerk voice this rule exists to convert. They are named because the model writes them when they are left unnamed:

- A sentence whose subject is the clause, the line, the unit, or the page  
- “The printed English…” / “This sentence does not…” / “The speech is happening on the floor of the feast”  
- A label standing in for a sentence (“Draw is water-work”)  
- A house calque after the Barrier has already spent the word (“relied into,” “honor-weight,” “came to be” stacked as a verb lesson)  
- A closing stamp that repeats the Barrier  
- A tour of what the speaker did not say, when the spoken word is already on the page  
- A fragment that only strikes a word already spoken (“His body.” / “I will raise it.” / “You.” as a sentence by itself after the hearing has already landed)  
- A line that could be spoken only by someone who has the Greek open

Write the meaning instead.

**Agency: the English carries the Greek's doing**

Thought includes agency: who acts, who is acted upon, whether it is ongoing or once. When the Greek verb's voice, tense, or mood carries that — the passive imperative that makes transformation something done to us, the present that makes it continuous, the dative that names our part — the English must carry it explicitly, in ordinary words. A smooth English sentence that drops the passive is not a simplification; it is a mistranslation. The drafter is the user's Greek speaker and English speaker both, and this rule pushes the English toward the Greek as far as honesty allows.

Good: "God does the transforming; the renewing of the mind is yours to do." Wooden: "You are being transformed (passive — done to you, and ongoing), and you must renew your mind (that part is yours)." Carry the agency; do not narrate the grammar.

**The trigger test.** For each verb, ask: if the English names who-does-what and the Greek's agency, does the sentence mean something different than the smooth version? If yes, the grammar carries theology and the English must carry it. If the sentence means the same either way, the grammar is machinery — leave it inside.

The push stops where the grammar stops. Where the Greek genuinely leaves the agency open, the fork stays open under Rule 2.68 — never manufacture certainty to satisfy this rule. And never hedge where the grammar is decisive: "both hearings stand" is forbidden where the verb has already decided. Push hard; fake nothing.

### 2.1 \[STAGE 3 WORKER & VALIDATOR\] Convert the audit into meaning

Stage 2 names what the English flattened, dropped, or rewired. Stage 3 turns that knowledge into thought-for-thought meaning: what happened, through whom, from where, and why it matters to the reader. The ordinary reader receives the force.

A Stage 3 group is finished when the load-bearing force named in Stage 2 is present in the teaching English and the Rules 2.05 finish test is met. Presence of the force is required. It is not sufficient.

Stage 3 is the meaning of the text. These three substitutes are other jobs:

- **Audit lecture** — explaining mismatches, ranges, particles, and manuscript lines as if the reader were in Stage 2\.  
- **Bare restatement** — repeating the verse as a fact (“The Law was given through Moses”) with no teaching of what that giving is.  
- **Discourse-architecture** — teaching the nature of the text instead of the meaning of the text.

The third was named on John 1:17. Stage 2 correctly records that ὅτι grounds 1:16 and does not open a new unit. That fact is working equipment. It is not a paragraph. Writing “The next line is not a new subject. It is the ground under that receiving.” tells the reader how a clause works and never says the because.

If ὅτι means because, state the because-content. If a verb is come-to-be rather than realized, tell the event. If a Witness scene is the meaning-world of the verse, teach inside that scene.

Translation Barriers may still name the printed English word and give its force. That is their job. The teaching after the Barrier spends that force as story.

**Wrong / right for the John 1:17 hinge**

Wrong: “We took from His fullness. The next line is not a new subject. It is the ground under that receiving.” Right: “We took from His fullness because God has never given Himself to this people except through a man He used. Through Moses He handed them His word. Through Jesus Christ the favor and the faithfulness themselves came to be.”

The second sentence used the ὅτι. The first sentence displayed it.

Barriers stay thick enough to carry the mismatches. Lexical pedantry remains the well. A new chat is not the remedy for a conversion failure. The remedy is this conversion.

### 2.2 \[STAGE 3 WORKER & VALIDATOR\] Lead with what the sentence means

Teach what the clause means, with a clear picture of what it is. Show the genuine. If a familiar English reading clashes with that meaning, the clash will arise in the reader.

Translation Barriers may still mark the printed English word. Running teaching shows the meaning.

Name the verse and the thing meant. Unpack the thought in the teaching itself.

### 2.3 \[STAGE 3 WORKER & VALIDATOR\] Grammar force becomes meaning

In Stage 3 teaching, the force of the grammar is converted into pure meaning so that a reader who has never studied Greek still receives the full weight of the point.

Technical labels belong only:

- Inside Translation Barriers, or  
- In a brief hybrid gloss that is immediately followed by the plain-English force (e.g., “the Greek word *hina*, which simply means ‘so that’ or ‘in order that’”).

These labels stay inside the AI when the teaching is written: aorist, perfect, present participle, qualitative relative, aorist subjunctive, punctiliar, and any similar grammar-lesson phrasing.

Ordinary readers have no interlinear. They receive what the text is saying and why it matters.

### 2.4 \[STAGE 3 WORKER & VALIDATOR\] Greek in the teaching is always glossed

When a Greek or Hebrew term appears in the teaching prose, it is transliterated (never in Greek or Hebrew letters) and paired with its plain-English sense in the same sentence.

Preferred form:

- *eis* (into)  
- or “into” (*eis*)

The readers of these documents treat their English Bible as the Word of God. They receive the meaning. A bare Greek form or transliteration standing alone in the teaching is equipment left on the table.

### 2.5 \[STAGE 3 WORKER & VALIDATOR\] Meaning gloss for overloaded English

Prefer a plain-English meaning gloss, not a Greek gloss.

The Teaching Bridge starts with the word the reader already sees on the page of their English Bible. That word is often an ecclesiastical shell. Put the recovered meaning beside the familiar word at once.

Model form:

- baptized (immersed into)

The parentheses do meaning-work: the action and, where the Greek carries it, the destination.

Use the familiar word so the reader knows which line is being opened. After that first meeting, let the meaning-English carry the paragraph.

The same pattern applies to other high-occupancy English terms when they appear in running teaching, for example:

- church (assembly)  
- cross (execution stake)  
- justified (cleared and set right)  
- the sin (the self-preserving principle we died to; it can still press claims through the mortal body, not as a second self living inside us)

Forms that leave the shell closed, or hang only a Greek label on it, are not the gloss:

- baptized  
- baptized (*baptizō*)  
- *baptizō*  
- baptized (βαπτίζω)

Translation Barriers may be fuller, because that is where load-bearing terms are unpacked. Even there, lead with meaning-English. Greek or Hebrew, transliterated, may appear in a Barrier to support the explanation, but the first clause the reader receives is the thought-for-thought sense.

### 2.6 \[STAGE 3 WORKER & VALIDATOR\] How a Stage 3 piece is cut

Stage 1 is the Puzzle: the section-shaped holes, already marked. A Stage 3 group is one piece. The job is to cut that piece so it is true, complete, and ready to set down later. Integration is a later job.

The surrounding verses are not unknown. Use that knowledge so this piece does not lie about where it sits. Write these verses only. Rulings about the adjoining verses belong to the chat that holds the whole chapter (Rules 2.66).

- If this group opens a Stage 1 section, start with the section title and write the thought-for-thought teaching of these verses only.  
- If this group continues a section already opened, give it a paragraph theme-title (when helpful) and write the teaching of these verses only.  
- If this group closes the section, do the same as a middle group. The integrated section-ending belongs to Integration.

Theme-titles name the thought, not the wording of the verse. The first sentence of the teaching is new speech, not an echo of the heading or the verse.

Every group leaves a complete dataset: thought-for-thought English of the Greek or Hebrew in view, Translation Barriers only where Rules 2.65 allows them, and only the intra-book Witnesses that are genuinely apropos to this piece. The thought-for-thought English must pass the Rules 2.05 finish test.

Trust the hole. Cut this piece. Stop.

### 2.65 \[STAGE 3 WORKER & INTEGRATION\] Stage 1 owns the page; Stage 2 does not

Stage 2 processing groups are audit labor. They exist so the Greek can be checked in 2-verse groups. They are not the outline. They are not the reader’s major sections. They are not a reason to print a Translation Barrier.

**What the teaching page follows**

- Stage 1 major movements become the Roman-numeral sections (`## I.`, `## II.`).  
- Stage 1 internal beats may become bold thematic titles inside that section, when a title helps the reader anticipate the subject or keeps a long teaching from becoming a wall. Those beats are content turns. A beat does not require a title.  
- If a Stage 2 group boundary and a Stage 1 beat disagree, the Stage 1 beat wins on the page. John 2 is the model: the outline's 2:3–5 and 2:6–8 govern; the audit's 2:3–4 and 2:5–6 do not.

Stage 3 may still be written group by group. That is labor. Integration assembles onto the Stage 1 frame. It does not concatenate audit folders and call the result a chapter.

**What a Translation Barrier is for**

A Barrier line is allowed only when at least one of these is true:

- The printed English flattens, distorts, or reroutes the force of the Greek (or Hebrew).  
- The printed word is a high-occupancy shell that will take the ordinary reader the wrong way unless the first-century meaning stands next to it.

A Stage 2 Part A notice is not enough. Stage 2 may mark a word because it is worth watching. If the supplied English version already said that word truly, the mark stays in the audit. It does not become a glossary line. If the reader still needs the picture — a wedding as a multi-day feast, the third day sitting on chapter 1 — that picture belongs in the teaching sentences, not in a Barrier that pretends the English was wrong.

These Barrier lines are named because the model writes them when they are left unnamed:

- A reprint of the verse in heavier words (*invited* — called into the feast)  
- A label for a name the English already got right (*the mother of Jesus* — His mother)  
- A stamp that only repeats the heading or the outline beat  
- A full Barrier list under a cluster whose English has no real problem

No Barrier list is required. An empty Barrier under 2:1–2 is correct when the teaching has already spent the day-count and the feast. The Style Guide already says these tools serve understanding; they are not a slot that must be filled.

### 2.7 \[STAGE 3 WORKER & VALIDATOR\] Verse numbers in quotation blocks

Every quotation block of the passage under study prints the verse numbers inside the block, in this style:

> 19 This is the testimony… 20 And he confessed…

Do this for every cluster, including the opening verses and single-verse blocks. The heading or line above may still name the range. A reader who is mid-page can see which verses are in the block.

**Why verse numbers stay inside the quote block even when the range is printed above it (Never strip them out as "redundant"):** The Translation Barrier and the teaching regularly refer to what happens in a specific verse within the block (e.g., contrasting *every* in 3:1 with *every* in 3:2). Without the plain numbers `1`, `2` sitting inside the quote text itself, the human reader cannot see where verse 1 ends and verse 2 begins.

Unbroken Witness quotations keep their own book-and-verse labels in the Witness heading.

### 2.8 \[STAGE 3 WORKER & VALIDATOR\] Witness world — intra-book spending stays; cross-book Witnesses are proposed at Integration

Stage 2 studies language. Stage 3 teaches meaning. Those are not the same labor.

A Stage 2 “not routed” list is equipment. It keeps this group on these verses. The same is true of the audit as a whole: starting point, not limit (Rules 2.0).

**Stage 3 may freely spend what belongs to the book already under the reader** — what the reader has already been given in this book: earlier chapters and earlier sections already read. "Book in hand" means what the study of this book already holds — the dossier, the outline, earlier chapters already read (e.g., Titus 1 and Titus 2 when teaching Titus 3). It is not a license to open a later chapter. If this writer has already taught a verb, a title, a construction, or a meaning-world earlier in the book, and this group uses it, the kinship registers — even when the audit did not print the parallel. **Spend the meaning naturally without clerk-voice disclaimers (Rules 2.0).** The founding cases: John 2:9’s γεγενημένον against John 1’s ἐγένετο, and Titus 3:4’s `τοῦ σωτῆρος ἡμῶν θεοῦ` against Titus 1:3–4 and 2:10–13. Unbroken Witness blocks drawn from earlier in the book already under the reader may be placed in Stage 3\.

**The letter's "and."** The audit records a fork as open at the verse level; that is its job, and it does not close it. But the letter's own verses, "and"-ed together, may close at the book level what one verse leaves open. Where the same writer's same title-form, verb, or construction across the letter's passages leaves one coherent reading — Paul's paired Savior titles across Titus 1, 2, and 3; John's come-to-be verbs across John 1 and 2 — the teaching may state the letter's reading, naming the passages it stands on. The claim is the letter's, evidenced by its verses: not the verse's alone, and not a verdict imported from outside the letter. What neither the verse nor the letter closes stays open.

What Stage 3 may not do is place Witnesses from *other* canonical books. A cross-book parallel the audit already knows — Matthew's "Spirit of God" beside John's "the spirit," the failed healing across the Synoptics, Judges 11 or Psalm 68 beside John 2 — may be parked for Integration, where it is placed under Rules 3.2 after the original-language check and the user's review.

Each writer keeps his own sentence. Set parallels beside each other so the event is richer — never to prove that one writer’s clause contains another’s words.

Naming is not opening, and naming is not identifying: no verse is selected, marked, or proposed as a witness candidate in Stage 2.

### 2.9 \[ALL STAGES\] Majority-report warnings for high-risk terms

The training data is heavily saturated with the common English meanings of certain words and pictures. Even when the project definitions are known, the default pull remains strong. These terms and images require active anticipation every time they appear.

**“the sin” / ἡ ἁμαρτία**

- Default English reading: a string of individual moral acts or a general moral category.  
- Project force in Romans 5–8: the personified internal principle of independent self-preservation and self-reliance that operates in the flesh when cut off from God. It is not a foreign demonic power and not merely discrete acts.  
- Anticipate the majority-report default and bridge from it.

**“baptism / baptized / immersion”**

- Default English reading: water immersion as a ritual or public symbol of commitment.  
- Project force in Romans 6:3–4 (and parallel texts): the decisive act by which the Father places / immerses the person into the Messiah Himself and into His death (Rom 6:3 glosses εἰς Χριστόν as εἰς τὸν θάνατον αὐτοῦ). Where Scripture names a transfer, its object is the Son’s kingdom (Col 1:13: rescued out of the authority of darkness, transferred into the kingdom of the beloved Son) — not a transfer of Christ’s history to us.  
- In running teaching, meet the reader’s printed word with the meaning gloss: **baptized (immersed into)**.

**Dual nature / “squatter” / second self in the body**

- Default Christian picture: a second nature (or an “old man / squatter”) still living inside the believer’s body as a resident occupant.  
- Project force: We do not have a dual nature. The body is simply still mortal and unredeemed. The old principle of independent self-preservation can still press residual claims through the mortal members, and the mind is being renewed as we bring every thought captive to the obedience of Christ.  
- The teaching speaks of a mortal body and a crucified self-life. It does not speak of a second self or an occupant living inside the believer.

**“Old master” / alien owner / possession (Romans 6 slavery language)**

- Default leak: “the old master” or “refusing the old master’s use of our members” sounds like we were possessed by someone other than ourselves — a foreign owner, a demon, or a tenant who still wants the house.  
- Project force: Paul’s slavery language is real (slaves of the sin / slaves of God and of righteousness). The bond that death broke was our own self-centered life — the former self-preserving person we were when cut off from God. That life was crucified with Christ. What remains is a mortal body that can still be offered the old way. The live question is whether we will hand the members back to a way of being ourselves that death already ended.  
- Running teaching keeps the slave as *us* and the crucified reality as *the self-life we were*. Avoid “old master,” “the old owner,” “evict the occupant.”

Model sentences:

- We refuse to put the mortal members back in service of the self-centered life that already died with Him.
- We used to live as slaves of that self-preserving life. That life was crucified with Christ. We will not hand the body back to the way of being ourselves that death already ended.
- We can still yield to the cravings that speak through the mortal body, or we can present ourselves to God as already alive from the dead and put the members in His service. That is the obedience of faith, not the remodeling of a self. The walk may grow steadier; that is not a change from unholy to holy.

Presenting order: Romans 6 says not to let that old self-preserving life reign in the mortal body so that we obey its desires; not to present the members as weapons to that life; to present *yourselves* to God as people already living from the dead, and the members as weapons of righteousness to God. The “self” in view is us. The members are the instruments. Teaching prose uses Paul’s “yourselves.”

Related contrast, not a new creed: Romans 6, Romans 7:4–6, Hebrews 10, and Galatians 5 are transfer-and-belonging texts. We put to death the deeds of the body because we already died to the sin and are alive to God. Behavior shows whether we are yielding to the mortal body’s cravings or presenting ourselves to God. It does not create the ownership, and it does not reform an unholy self into a holy one.

### 2.66 \[CHAPTER ORCHESTRATOR / INTEGRATION\] The chat that holds the whole chapter checks the boundaries

A group working chat opens its own 2 verses (plus any shared sentence header and the book already under the reader). Still, no isolated group chat sees how a later group in the same chapter was drafted. The chat that holds the whole chapter — the Orchestrator or Integration chat that divided the work and knows where the boundaries fall — checks every sense-decision (every gloss, Barrier line, and rendering choice that fixes a word's sense) against the adjoining verses on both sides of each boundary before the work is approved.

Where a Stage 2 boundary cuts a continuous Greek or Hebrew sentence, the Orchestrator supplies the full sentence to both groups as visible syntactic context (Rule 1.0) and resolves all sense-decisions across the entire sentence before approval.

It looks for three things: a gloss that narrows a meaning the next verse keeps open, a denial the adjoining verse would not sustain, a vividness the clause did not supply.

The founding case is Revelation 1: a group read ἐν τάχει as manner only — swiftly, not soon — and the next verse says the time is near (ἐγγύς). One verse outside the group's allowlist. Invisible to the working chat. Decisive for the meaning.

When a chapter arrives as one Working Draft pasted by the user (rather than assembled from Group files through the pipeline), the same chat is the chapter chat: it runs this check before approval, not after a later Integration.

### 2.67 \[STAGE 3 WORKER & VALIDATOR\] Stage 3 keeps the plain reading

Stage 3 converts the audit. It does not drop its flags, override its restraints, or close a genuine lexical range that both the Greek clause and intra-book context leave standing. Rules 2.0 and 2.8 still hold: book-knowledge already under the reader is freely spent. It may not be used to cancel what this group's verses actually say. These restraints hold:

- Every audit flag (Part A and Part B) is honored in the teaching English or explicitly answered. A flag the audit raised does not go quiet in the prose.  
- A restraint the audit names stays a restraint. "Do not import marketplace language" is an instruction to the writer, not a denial to preach from the pulpit.  
- The teaching adds no participants and no objects the clause does not supply. The men at the cross are not in ἐξεκέντησαν; the verb says "those who pierced him."  
- A puzzling expression is rendered as written. "Seven spirits" stays seven spirits. A harmonization — "sevenfold Spirit" — is labeled as supplied, never as the text.  
- Likeness stays likeness, background stays background, repetition stays repetition, sequence stays sequence. A sash like a priest's is not a priesthood; imperial color around "the Lord's day" is background, not lexical meaning; "he turned to see" then "having turned, he saw" is two steps, not one.  
- The teaching claims no grammatical force the audit did not establish. Rhetoric does not manufacture morphology: ἄρχων is a present-office title whether or not the verb still moves.

### 2.68 \[STAGE 2 WORKER ONLY\] Stage 2 does not close what its verses leave open (Genuine Forks vs. Post-95 AD Drift)

- **Genuine first-century forks stay open:** Where the first-century lexical range permits two real readings within the 30–95 AD baseline (e.g., ἐν τάχει as swiftness and nearness, or φυλαὶ τῆς γῆς as tribes of the land or of the earth), the Stage 2 audit keeps both open and does not make an either/or judgment that depends on verses outside the group.  
- **The letter's "and" is not the audit's job:** A fork the audit keeps open at the verse level may be closed at the book level by the letter's own verses "and"-ed together (Rule 2.8). The audit does not perform that closing; it records the fork as open and lets the book-level rule do its work. Post-95 AD dogmatic projections in modern lexicons are set aside under the 30–95 AD baseline (Knowledge Base, Dual Function — Sections I–III only) — but the baseline is a probability lens, not a verdict: what neither the verse nor the letter closes stays open.

### 2.69 \[CHAPTER ORCHESTRATOR / INTEGRATION\] Review checks before a stage is approved

Before Stage 3 is approved, the whole-chapter chat runs these checks on every Group file:

1. The Rules 2.05 first-century restatement was accepted by the user before Barriers and teaching were written.  
2. Every audit flag (Part A and Part B) the audit raised is honored or explicitly answered in the prose.  
3. The boundary check (Rules 2.66) is done at every group boundary.  
4. Repeated phrases across the chapter and book are compared word for word. A changed small word — ἐν become ἐπί — is noticed, not smoothed; an identical title phrase — `τοῦ σωτῆρος ἡμῶν θεοῦ` — is kept consistent.  
5. Every Barrier headword is the index pair (Rules 2.72) whose first element matches a bolded term in the quoted passage it stands over. A Barrier whose rendering keeps the word unchanged carries the single word.  
6. Material composed during integration goes through the same checks. Assembly is not exempt.

### 2.70 \[STAGE 2 WORKER ONLY\] Stage 2 never opens text outside its group

**What counts as a leak:** A leak is importing a fact from outside the book under study. Spending the meaning of the book already under the reader — a title, verb, or household relationship the author has established earlier in the same book — is exegesis, not a leak (Rule 2.8). **Stage 2 audits only its assigned 2-verse group (plus any shared sentence header) and routes intra-book anchors; Stage 3 spends the book already under the reader (Rules 2.8, 2.71).**

The Stage 2 audit's value is that it sees only its verses (and its shared sentence header where a group boundary cuts a continuous sentence). No other biblical text outside this group is opened, looked up, quoted, or checked in Stage 2 — not for confirmation, not for contrast. The Stage 2 worker holds the group’s task packet — the group’s verses, the Stage 1 location extract (Rule 1.2), the Knowledge Base as Rule 1.2 delivers it (the Claim floor and the forced entries printed in the packet, the rest by logged retrieval), and the lexicon shelf — nothing else from the canon.

What stays: the audit still routes anchor lexemes for this group only, identified from the book already under the reader. That routing is equipment (Rules 2.8), not bleed — it names load-bearing Greek words as routing leads; it does not open text. Naming is not opening, and naming is not identifying: no verse is selected, marked, or proposed as a witness candidate in Stage 2. What moves: proposing cross-book witness verses, with the original-language check and the user's review of the complete Working Draft, belongs to Integration (Rules 3.2). Accept thinner routing as the trade: a named echo lives or dies at Integration's original-language check (Rules 3.2).

### 2.71 \[STAGE 3 WORKER & VALIDATOR\] Teaching is exegesis; exposition is a later layer

Stage 3 writes *teaching*: what these verses say, in warm thought-for-thought English, under the restraints of Rules 2.67. That is still exegesis — the claim being made is "this is what the text says." Stage 3 teaching is what the text says. `Alongside this` is what the teacher hears. A section with no hearing gets no unit. The page is complete without it.

*Exposition* is what the teacher hears beyond the book under study: cross-book canonical echoes, contemporary illustrations, insights, and applications that arise in teaching. Exposition is not written in Stage 3\. It is proposed at Integration under Rules 3.3, labeled as exposition, checked by the pushback protocol, and admitted only through collaborative dialogue and the user's review of the complete Working Draft.

Same voice, different claim. The label tells the reader which is speaking. Exposition never masquerades as exegesis; without the label, the framework loses the distinction that makes it trustworthy.

**Register test.** Three claims, three homes:

- "This is what these verses say (in the flow of the book already read)" → teaching; must survive Rules 2.67 and 2.8.  
- "Scripture says the same thing over here" → witness candidate; intra-book witnesses from earlier in the same book are allowed in Stage 3 (Rule 2.8); cross-book witnesses must wait for Integration and survive Rules 3.2.  
- "This is what I hear once those verses are granted" → exposition candidate; must wait for Integration and survive Rules 3.3.

**Detecting the leak (Intra-Book Continuity Is NOT a Leak).** The test is not introspective — do not ask what the paragraph felt like to write. Read each paragraph and ask whether a reader holding these verses *within the flow of the chapters already read in this same book (Rule 2.8)* and *the complete Greek/Hebrew sentence in which these verses sit (Rule 2.66)* could tell this is what they say. **Intra-book continuity is exegesis, not a leak:** when an author repeats a phrase, title, verb, or household relationship he already established earlier in the same book (such as Paul's paired Savior titles across Titus 1, 2, and 3), recognizing and spending that continuity belongs to exegesis without any disclaimer label. If the paragraph needs a fact from *outside this book* — another canonical book, a modern life application, or an external homiletic image — it is exposition, whatever it felt like. Mark it and park it for Integration.

**Parking candidates.** If a cross-book echo or an exposition candidate surfaces during Stage 3 work — the run, the QC, or the user's read-through — do not write a teaching-note file, and do not put it in the Group file. Leave it for the Integration dialogue. Capture is not placement. Nothing parked enters the teaching.

### 2.72 \[STAGE 3 WORKER & VALIDATOR\] The bold chain is an index of meaning

Where a Translation Barrier exists, the printed English word and the rendering's word for it will differ — that difference is what the Barrier is. The bold marks the meaning, not identical strings:

- In the quoted text, bold the minimal English word(s) whose meaning is under review — the words that differ, not the whole phrase ("spirits," not "the seven spirits of God").  
- In **What it says**, bold the minimal rendering word(s) used for each bold word in the quotation ("breaths"). If it takes a short phrase to say it, bold that phrase.  
- The Barrier headword is the index pair: **quote-word** / **rendering-word** ("**spirits** / **breaths**," "**Spirit** / **breath**," "**came** / **stream out**," "**cease** / **never stop**"). The pair shows the reader that two different visible words carry one meaning. Where the rendering keeps the word unchanged, the headword is the single word ("**crowns**").  
- Grammatical observations — the article's absence, number, case — are exegesis for the Barrier body, not part of the pair. The pair carries lexical meaning only.

**Why this index-pair is essential for the human reader (Never drop or "simplify" it):** The Translation Barrier is the visual key that bridges the printed English Bible to **What it says**. Often an English translation uses the exact same English word twice in one sentence for two different Greek meanings (e.g., Titus 3:1–2 **"every"** → **"each"** in 3:1, but **"every"** → **"in every instance and in full measure"** in 3:2), or swaps English words across two different Greek roots (e.g., Titus 3:2 translating *epieikeis* as **"gentle"** and *prautēta* as **"consideration,"** so when **What it says** restores *prautēta* to **"gentleness,"** the reader would think **"gentleness"** came from **"gentle"** unless the Barrier maps both sides: `**gentle**/**gracious, forbearing, fair-minded**` and `**consideration**/**gentleness**`). Without both sides of the slash-pair bolded in the Barrier headword, the human reader cannot track which bold word in the NASB maps to which bold word/phrase in **What it says**.

## 3\. Integration (Collaborative Whole-Chapter Synthesis)

Integration begins after every Stage 3 group for the passage exists.

**Integration is an interactive, human-collaborative dialogue — never a silent automated script.** An agentic AI must never treat Integration as a purely mechanical file-stitching job that shuns conversation. After assembling the Working Draft, the AI presents the draft and actively engages the user in dialogue to resolve any cross-group questions, evaluate cross-book `### Unbroken Witness` candidates, and invite/shape `### Alongside this` exposition units.

### 3.0 \[CHAPTER ORCHESTRATOR / INTEGRATION\] Integration — post-stage labor

**Names.** A **Group file** is the approved Stage 3 file for one processing group; until the user approves it, it is the Stage 3 piece. The **Working Draft** is the single chapter file Integration assembles from the Group files and refines with the user. The **Published Exegesis** is the finished, titled Working Draft. Every Framework file uses these three names for these three things.

Integration runs in this order:

1. **Assemble the complete Working Draft.** Place the approved groups onto the Stage 1 frame. The operations:  
   - (a) chapter title;  
   - (b) a fresh "how did we get here?" `## Apostolic Lens`;  
   - (c) the Stage 1 Roman-numeral section frame, with group splits routed at section boundaries;  
   - (d) distributed quotation presentations and tight Barrier treatments at the joints of each section;  
   - (e) continuous seam-dissolved teaching across the groups — re-orienting openings dissolved, only no-new-hearing transitions admitted;  
   - (f) `### Unbroken Witness` sections proposed and placed where they add to the explicit meaning of the Greek or Hebrew text (Rules 3.2);  
   - (g) any teacher hearing produced in the Integration dialogue with the user, woven into a labeled `### Alongside this` unit where Rules 3.3 place it. A section with no hearing gets none. One Working Draft.  
   **The Working Draft holds teaching only.** First-century and dictionary checks and traceability notes live in their own files, as reference for future exegesis — they are never written into the Working Draft. Worker notes and open items likewise live in the worker's own file, never in the Working Draft.

2. **Essential-prose pass** on the draft. A different test from Stage 3: *if this sentence were gone, would the reader lose a meaning the chapter actually needs?*  
3. **Interactive Dialogue & Review with the User.** The AI presents the draft and actively invites the user's feedback, questions, Witness ideas, and expositional hearings. What the user does not question stands; what he questions is discussed, refined, or amended together. Authorization to finalize is the user's explicit word in chat — "Integration draft approved," or an unmistakable equivalent such as "publish it."

Review handling: answer each question against the Stage 2/3 artifacts before diagnosing — a correction that fails an artifact's check sends the fix to the failed stage's file first, then to the draft.

What the essential-prose pass does:

- Leaves each new unit to begin as itself.  
- Leaves a unit finished when the teaching of these verses has been told.  
- Keeps a majority-report correction at first arrival (first appearance in the Working Draft's reading order). Later units may point back in a clause.  
- Where a word unpacked in an earlier Barrier returns in a later unit with a local translation flattening of its own, keep a brief Barrier bullet for that local flattening, with a one-line pointer back to the verse where the word first arrived.  
- Leaves the writer’s own returns audible. Those are the argument, not scaffolding.  
- Leaves Translation Barriers only where Rules 2.65 still allows them. Drop a Barrier that is only a Stage 2 notice. The teaching spends the meaning.  
- Lets method-tics drop once the reader already knows the move (“Start with the words already on the page,” “Meet the printed English”).

These cuts are named because the model writes them when they are left unnamed:

- A recap bridge that retells the previous cluster before the new one is allowed to begin  
- A closing stamp that reprints the unit just taught  
- A Barrier line that is about the page instead of the room  
- A subsection that exists only because it was a Stage 2 processing group  
- A Barrier list that reprints the audit under words the supplied English version already said

If a line cannot be judged as recap or load-bearing, leave it and name it. Do not guess-cut.

Default page form after assembly: the Stage 1 section is the unit. The section names its whole range once, at the open. Actual quotations are distributed at the joints, each with its own Barrier, so the section does not become one Barrier farm. Internal beats may carry thematic titles — the paragraph-group titles — inside that section. Two Stage 3 groups that fall inside one Stage 1 beat become one cluster, not two mini-chapters. Each Witness follows the teaching of the verse block it illuminates; a Witness serving the whole section follows the final block. Style Guide section 8 is the place.

Page count is not a virtue. Necessity is.

**Integration inputs — assembly & dialogue**

Integration uses:

- The Framework constitution already loaded.  
- This Standing Rules file, section 3 first.  
- The Stage 1 Structural Outline as the board.  
- The Group files for this passage (and Stage 2 audits when checking a review question or Witness anchor).  
- The Exegesis Style and Format Guide for page shape only.  
- The Generated Knowledge Base, Sections I–III only.

### 3.1 \[CHAPTER ORCHESTRATOR / INTEGRATION\] Conversion pass on a Working Draft

This is still Integration. It is used when Stage 3 groups exist, a Working Draft has been assembled, and the cup failed: the hearings are present, but the teaching still talks like Stage 2\.

**The job**

Rules 2.05 is the finish test. This section is the hands. Take a Working Draft and convert named clusters or sections until an ordinary reader can see the first-century thought. Edit that file in place. One Working Draft.

Edit the named cluster in place. Do not rewrite the entire living file in one write.

**Conversion inputs — the named range only**

A conversion pass is written from these inputs only:

- This Standing Rules file — Rules 2.05 and 3.1 first.
- The Working Draft named by the user.
- The Stage 1 outline for location only.
- The Stage 2 Linguistic Audit for the cluster now in hand. That file is the well.
- The Exegesis Style and Format Guide for page shape only (verse block, Barrier, Witness, heading hierarchy). Do not imitate its sample voice.
- The Generated Knowledge Base, Sections I–III only, and only for already-flagged lexemes.
- An already-converted stretch of this same Working Draft, when the user names that stretch as the voice sample.

That is the complete set for a conversion pass.

A short user instruction is enough once this section is loaded. The user names the living file and the range.

**Finished test for a converted cluster**

The Rules 2.05 test. Plus: the cluster reads like the already-converted stretch of this same draft.

**How to cut one cluster**

- Open that cluster’s Stage 2 audit and internalize the load-bearing force.  
- Print the Rules 2.05 restatement in the chat. Halt until the user accepts it or amends it.  
- Rewrite the Barrier as meaning-English. Anticipation of a printed English word stays here. Say the meaning once.  
- Rewrite the teaching from the accepted restatement, in the voice of Rules 2.05. Close the audit as a voice source. Reopen it only to check that a named force was not dropped.  
- Keep the verse block, verse numbers, bold links, section title, theme-title, approved hearings, and Witnesses that still carry load.  
- Stop when the teaching of these verses has been told.

**Halt**

Convert only the range the user names. If the user names Section II, do that cluster and halt. A sweep of the rest of the chapter waits for the user to name that range.

### 3.2 \[CHAPTER ORCHESTRATOR / INTEGRATION\] Witnesses are placed at Integration

No stage before Integration places cross-book witness verses. Stage 2 never opens text outside its group (Rules 2.70); Stage 3 does not hunt the canon — its labor is converting the audit, and it spends only the book already under the reader (Rules 2.0, 2.8). Integration is the first point at which the whole chapter and all audits are held together as labor, and it is the same logic as Rules 2.66: only the whole-chapter view places across canonical boundaries.

**Placement.** For each section of the draft, the drafter proposes and places Witness sections from what the user names in the Integration dialogue, the drafter's own view of the whole chapter, and an audit's anchor routing where one exists — re-checked fresh, never carried over unexamined.

**Earlier placements.** A witness placed in an earlier section may be invoked in a later section by reference to that placement rather than re-quoted — e.g. 1 Pet 2:18-23 placed at 12:17-18, then invoked at 12:21. The later section's by-reference invocation is the record of the dependency; if the earlier placement is struck at review, the later section places it fresh (the fallback). In practice, work in chapter order so earlier placements are settled facts, not open dependencies.

**Approval.** The drafter places; the user reviews the complete Working Draft and questions what he disagrees with (Rules 3.0). During review, placed witnesses may carry a source label — the user's naming, the drafter's view, or prior audit routing — so the reviewer knows what he is reading; the labels are review metadata and are stripped from the Published Exegesis. No general disclaimer is printed under the Witness heading. The illumination criterion stays in the dialogue.

**The criterion.** A witness is placed only if it adds to the explicit meaning of the Greek or Hebrew text in view — it illuminates what the verses explicitly say. It does not decorate, does not import, does not settle what the verses leave open, except a 3.3 close, which may be quoted here and still may not replace what the verse says. Few, load-bearing: a section that needs six witnesses probably needs none of them. This is a criterion the drafter holds in dialogue, even when the suggestion is the user's. If a proposed witness does not add to what is written, the drafter pushes back and names the miss. The pushback is not a veto: the user decides. The sentence "Placed where they illuminate what the passage explicitly says" is this criterion. It is not printed under the Witness heading.

**The brake.** Default is zero cross-book Witnesses unless they genuinely illuminate a load-bearing Greek or Hebrew word in the passage. "Default zero" does not run on intra-book Witnesses from the book already under the reader.

**Original-language check.** Lexical questions go first to BDAG (Greek) and HALOT (Hebrew) — the default first look, still proposals, tested like any claim. The check runs at least at a surface level: verify that the key terms carrying the echo are really there. Meaning-witnesses must be visible in faithful English; wording-witnesses must hold in the original. This catches mirage echoes (the English suggests what the original does not say) and hidden echoes (the original supports more than the English shows). If the LXX or Hebrew text cannot be accessed, the drafter asks the user to supply it rather than proceeding on English alone.

### 3.3 \[CHAPTER ORCHESTRATOR / INTEGRATION\] Exposition enters at Integration, labeled

*Exposition* — what the teacher hears beyond the book under study: canonical rooms, contemporary images, insights, and pastoral applications that arise in teaching — enters at Integration under this rule, and it always carries its label (`### Alongside this`). The reader always knows which register is speaking.

**Active Collaborative Dialogue.** While `### Alongside this` is never mandatory for a block (absence is fine when no hearing arises), the AI at Integration must **welcome and facilitate dialogue** with the user to explore what the user hears in the passage and help shape those hearings into warm, impactful `### Alongside this` units. Never shun exposition dialogue out of rigid formalism.

**The pool.** The pool is the Integration dialogue (plus any thoughts the user voiced during Stage 3 and parked for Integration). The user speaks a hearing or insight, the AI discusses and drafts it collaboratively with the user, and it is woven into the draft.

**How Group N comments become page.** During Stage 3 Group N approval the user may speak in teacher voice or quote Scripture; the AI says whether that addition belongs to those verses. Agreed material is not written into the Group N Group file, and it is not saved as a note file. It is not input to Group N+1's write. If it is still wanted, it is spoken again in the Integration dialogue. Scripture that illuminates a word on the page → Unbroken Witness. A teacher hearing, if one is kept in the Integration dialogue, → that section's **`Alongside this`** unit, after the Witness block, marked once. No hearing, no unit. The user then reads the whole Working Draft and may still cut, move, or add, because the adjoining sections are visible.

**The gate: the "and" of Scripture.** An expositional insight may open a canonical room the verse leaves standing. It may not settle what the verse left open, and it may not add a claim the verse does not carry. It may not go against the text, and it may not go past it. The Lloyd-Jones test — does not go beyond, does not fall short; the "and" of Scripture — is the Rule 3.3 gate.

**Exception: one scripture closing another.** Where a verse leaves a question, a verdict, or a word open, or looks to have closed what another scripture closes, the hearing may close it from that text, in the Lens or in `### Alongside this`, when (1) that text explicitly says the closing thing, (2) the verse stays true ("all the verses 'and' together must be true"), and (3) the close is that text's claim, not a speculative system. The close may name the word the other text has closed. The exegesis register never closes; it reports what the verse says and leaves open what the verse leaves open.

**Pushback, not prohibition.** The check runs when the comment is proposed in the Integration dialogue: where the text supports the echo, where the echo would go beyond the text, whether the text leaves room for it, and whether the reader needs it to live the verse or the teacher is enjoying the verse. If the user suggests a hearing that does not add to what is written, the drafter pushes back and names the miss. The pushback is not a veto: the user decides. What survives the check may be taught; what does not is set aside or reframed. The woven unit goes neither against the text nor past it, except the 3.3 close, which is past the verse by permission and still not against it. The Lloyd-Jones test is this gate. It is not printed under the unit.

**Weaving.** If a hearing is kept, it is woven as one contained unit with the verse block whose hearing it carries: after that block's teaching, and after its Witness block if it has one. The heading is the fixed `### Alongside this` (no bold inside the heading), marked once per unit, never per sentence.

### 3.4 \[CHAPTER ORCHESTRATOR / INTEGRATION\] Re-rendering never re-adjudicates

A finished stretch is sometimes carried into a new form: Integration assembling the Group files into the Working Draft, the conversion pass re-prosing that draft, a later rendering of the same page. The new form inherits the decisions of its source. Open words stay open. The hearing stays out of the exegesis, and a close stays in the hearing. Carry each decision across first, then find the new wording for it. The assembler, the converter, and the later renderer do not silently re-decide in paraphrase. The user still amends. They do not.

A rendering in another tongue is not a new exegesis. No lexical work is redone. The original language is not reopened. The printed wording in that tongue is still checked against the decision already carried across. A right word does not end the check. An addition, a dropped picture, or a second idiom flattened into the first is still a Barrier. The decision already made is carried across, then the target wording is found for it. Verse quotations are copied from the Bible in that tongue, never translated from the English quotation. The user supplies that verse text. The drafter places it verbatim.

Review checks on any re-rendering:

1. Every restatement obeys the constraints stated alongside it.  
2. Nothing is silently settled that the source left genuinely open.  
3. The registers have not bled. The exegesis still ends with the verse's verdict. The hearing still stays alongside.  
4. Praise does not end the check. Where the source got it right, the check continues past the nod.

## 4\. Why this matters

Grammar, original-language terms, and high-frequency English words all exist for meaning. When the teaching stops to name the machinery, leaves an unglossed Greek word, lets an overloaded English shell stand alone, allows a majority-report default to stand unchallenged, or teaches the architecture of the page instead of the meaning the architecture was found to carry, the reader is pulled out of the living force of the passage.

The 1st-Century Contextual Framework and the Teaching Bridge both require that the final prose remain warm, eye-level, and impactful for ordinary believers. Pedantic Stage 2 data remains the well. It is never the drink.
